# Source Register

**Snapshot date:** 2026-10-23  
**Scope:** sources used to design the nine skills, the core behavior contract, the product passport, and the scenario corpus.  
**Status:** a curated register, not a systematic review or meta-analysis. No source here proves that this repository improves sales outcomes.

---

## 1. Method

1. Prefer primary or official material: peer-reviewed papers, standards, regulator guidance, vendor documentation, and the local Hermes installation.
2. Record vendor and practitioner material as such. A widely sold sales book is not a peer-reviewed study.
3. Separate a source's **existence and wording** from its **truth**. Fetching a page verifies what the publisher claims, not that the claim is correct.
4. Keep direct quotation minimal. Paraphrase unless the exact wording matters.
5. Do not use a source to justify a tactic that conflicts with consent, truthfulness, or the buyer's interests.
6. Record access failures instead of silently substituting a summary.
7. Re-check prices, policies, and technical behavior in the client's own environment before production.

### Evidence grades

| Grade | Meaning | How this repository uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Peer-reviewed research or a formal standard, with a stated scope | Supports a narrow technical or behavioral claim, with limits |
| **A2** | Official technical or regulator documentation, verified directly | Supports format, API, or compliance-direction claims |
| **B** | Peer-reviewed but contested, context-bound, or replication-limited | Used cautiously, never as a universal sales law |
| **C** | Vendor, consultancy, or proprietary research with a commercial interest | Used as a hypothesis or vocabulary, not proof of causality |
| **D** | Practitioner book or framework based on experience | Used as a structured option, not evidence of effectiveness |
| **E** | Secondary summary, encyclopedia, or community repository | Orientation only; not load-bearing |
| **X** | Excluded for integrity or safety reasons | Not used in any skill |

A lower grade does not automatically mean "wrong." It means the repository does not treat the claim as established.

## 3. Access limitations and corrections

- **Gartner:** the 2025 press release was fetched successfully on 2026-10-23, including its sample size and claims. A related "B2B Buying Journey" page returned HTTP 403 on the same day. The repository treats the press release as a vendor survey, not as independent peer-reviewed evidence.
- **Books:** editions are cited, but this snapshot did not re-run a systematic literature search or obtain permission to quote at length. The repository paraphrases and keeps quotations short.
- **Vendor pages:** SPIN, Sandler, Cialdini, and Gap Selling pages are commercially interested. Their descriptions are used to represent the frameworks accurately, not to validate their outcome claims.
- **Hermes:** format behavior was checked against the local version only. The isolated loading test uses a temporary `HERMES_HOME`; it does not prove behavior in another version or in a production profile.
- **Kyrgyz and Russian:** no native-speaker review has been completed for this snapshot. The language examples are drafts, and the tests flag Kyrgyz/mixed scenarios for human review.
- **Legal:** the repository is not legal advice. The FTC guide is one jurisdiction's direction; consent, recording, privacy, refunds, and consumer-protection rules must be reviewed for the actual market.
- **Sales outcome claims:** no source in this register shows that these skills increase revenue, close rates, or retention. That would require a controlled experiment with this implementation.
- **Method coverage gaps:** Oren Klaff's framing vocabulary (S26) was a known gap in the first snapshot. It is now recorded but not adopted: the repository uses only the integrity-compatible idea of reducing perceived risk through verified clarity, and rejects frame-control and status tactics. Adding a method because it is famous is not evidence that it improves behavior.
- **Consumer and adoption evidence:** the repository's consumer and adoption guidance is a product decision, not a cited finding. Consumer cooling-off law and adoption-success research were not reviewed as primary sources for this snapshot; see the `unresolved` fields in the example passports.

## 4. Sources deliberately not used as proof

| Claim or source | Why not load-bearing |
|---|---|
| "Top 0.1% sales agent" | No benchmark, sample, or definition; aspirational only |
| "Always end every message with a question" | No evidence of universal benefit; can create pressure and interrogate the buyer |
| "7–38–55" or "93% of communication is nonverbal" | Popularized and commonly misapplied beyond the original studies; not used |
| "5–7 words reveal a personality type" | Unsupported psychotyping; high risk of discrimination and manipulation |
| "Alpha" / dominance-based selling | Not a validated construct for this context; conflicts with consent |
| Fake typing delays or fake human identity | Deception; not a legitimate engagement tactic |
| Invented scarcity or urgency | Conflicts with truthfulness; consumer-protection risk |
| Fabricated case studies, ROI, or guarantees | Misrepresentation; prohibited by the core contract |
| Jordan Belfort's "straight line" method | Excluded for integrity reasons; see S14 |
| Vendor "success rate" percentages without a dataset | Unverifiable; not used in skill logic or tests |

## 5. Maintenance

- Re-verify all URLs and editions before a public release.
- Re-run the Hermes loading test against each supported Hermes version.
- Add a source only with a grade and a stated limitation.
- If a claim in a skill cannot be traced to this register or to an explicit repository decision, remove the claim or label it as a decision.
- When a source is superseded, keep the old row with a `superseded` note rather than silently rewriting history.
